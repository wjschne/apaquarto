Thesis and Dissertation Tutorial

This tutorial is for graduate students who want to write a thesis or dissertation with apaquarto’s thesis mode. You do not need to be a programmer, and you do not need to have used tools like Quarto or RStudio before. If you can use a word processor and install a program, you can do this.

A few promises before we start:

  • You will not break anything. Every step can be repeated. If something goes sideways, you can almost always close the program, reopen it, and try again.
  • It is normal to feel lost at first. Setup is the hardest part. Once it is done, writing is much like writing in any other program.
  • Ask for help early. Your advisor, a labmate, or a campus technology help desk can often sort out an installation problem in minutes. The apaquarto issue tracker is also friendly to beginners’ questions.
ImportantCheck your program’s requirements

Thesis mode follows Temple University’s Dissertation and Thesis Handbook (see the options page for the edition it follows). Formatting rules change from year to year, and your department or committee may ask for something different. Before you submit, confirm the current requirements with the Graduate School and your committee. Treat apaquarto’s output as a very good first draft of the format, not as an official approval.

You do these steps once. After that, your writing routine is just: open the project, write, click Render.

NoteWhat are all these programs, and why so many?

Think of building a house. You need tools (R, Quarto), a workshop to use them in (RStudio), and a few specialty parts (packages and extensions).

Piece What it is Everyday analogy
Quarto The engine that turns a plain-text file into a finished document The printing press
apaquarto An add-on for Quarto that knows APA style and Temple’s thesis layout The house style the press is set up to follow
R A programming language A utility that RStudio plugs into
RStudio A free writing environment with Quarto built in Your desk
Zotero A free reference manager Your index-card box

You will write in a .qmd file (a “Quarto document”). It is a plain-text file with a little markup for things like italics and headings. You never format by hand: you tell Quarto what each piece of text is (a heading, a quotation, a citation) and apaquarto decides how it should look.

Before You Begin: Finding Your Way Around Your Computer

Many students keep their files mostly online in OneDrive and only open them through a browser. That works well for Word, but the programs in this tutorial need to find your files on your computer. This short section gives you the vocabulary you will need.

TipKey idea

A folder on your computer and a folder in OneDrive can be the same folder. OneDrive keeps a copy of a folder on your computer and copies it up to the cloud whenever it changes. You can look at it either way. Step 5 shows how to set this up.

Where are my files?

  • Windows
  • Mac
  • Linux

Open File Explorer (the yellow folder icon on your taskbar, or press Win+EWin+E). On the left you will see Documents, Desktop, and, if OneDrive is installed and signed in, an entry such as OneDrive – Temple University. That is your OneDrive folder, and it lives on your computer.

To see a folder’s full location, click once in the address bar at the top of File Explorer. It will look something like C:\Users\yourname\Documents\Thesis.

Open Finder (the smiley-face icon in the Dock). In the sidebar you will see Documents, Desktop, and, if the OneDrive app is installed and signed in, OneDrive. To see a folder’s full location, open it in Finder and choose View → Show Path Bar. The path appears at the bottom of the window.

Open your file manager (called Files on many systems). Your personal files live in your home folder, for example /home/yourname/Documents. Microsoft does not offer an official OneDrive app for Linux, so see the Linux note in Step 5.

What is the terminal?

A terminal is a window where you type a command instead of clicking a button. You will use one a few times. You never need to understand it deeply: you type (or paste) the exact text shown on this page, press EnterEnter, and read what comes back. RStudio has a terminal built in (a tab next to the Console), so you do not need to hunt for a separate program.

Step 1: Install R

R is a free programming language used for statistics. You do not have to use R for anything. apaquarto works perfectly well for a thesis that contains no analyses at all. We install R only because RStudio, the friendliest place to use Quarto, expects it to be there. If you do analyze data, you will already have R ready.

  • Windows
  • Mac
  • Linux
  1. Go to the R download page for Windows.
  2. Click Download R (the exact version number will differ from what you see here).
  3. Open the downloaded file and click through the installer. The default choices are fine.
  1. Go to the R download page for macOS.
  2. Choose the installer that matches your Mac. If you are not sure which you have, click the Apple menu and choose About This Mac. If it says Apple M1, M2, M3 (or similar), pick the Apple silicon version. If it says Intel, pick the Intel version.
  3. Open the downloaded .pkg file and click through the installer.

Instructions depend on your distribution. Start at the R download page for Linux and choose Debian, Fedora, Red Hat, SUSE, or Ubuntu. Those pages give the exact commands to paste into a terminal. The version of R in your system’s default software store is often old, so it is worth following the CRAN instructions.

You will not see a new program to open. That is fine. R works quietly in the background once RStudio is installed.

Step 2: Install RStudio

RStudio is the program you will write in. It is free.

  1. Go to the RStudio Desktop download page.
  2. Choose the installer for your system (Windows, macOS, or your Linux distribution) and install it as you would any other program.
  3. Open RStudio. The first time, it should find R on its own.
TipWhat if I already use another program, such as Positron or VS Code?

RStudio is only one of several places to use Quarto. If you already like another one, use it. Positron is a newer program from the same company that makes RStudio and Quarto, and it works with many languages. VS Code is another popular option. The steps in this tutorial are written for RStudio, because it is the gentlest place to begin, but the ideas carry over. See the installation page for more options.

A quick tour of RStudio

When RStudio opens, you will see several panes (windows within the window). Their exact arrangement may differ on your screen, but you will mostly care about these:

  • Source (top left, once you open a file): where you write.
  • Console and Terminal (bottom left, as tabs): where you type commands.
  • Files, Packages, and Viewer (right side, as tabs): where you see your folder, installed add-ons, and a preview of your document.

If you cannot find the Terminal tab, choose Tools → Terminal → New Terminal from the menu at the top (Mac users: the menu bar at the top of the screen).

Step 3: Install the Packages apaquarto Needs

A package is a small add-on for R. apaquarto uses a couple of them to make clean, sharp figures. You install each package once.

  1. In RStudio, click in the Console (the pane with a > symbol).
  2. Paste the following line and press EnterEnter:
Console
install.packages(c("ragg", "svglite", "quarto"))
  1. Wait. Text will scroll by for a minute or two. When the > symbol returns, it is finished.

If a message asks whether you would like to install packages from source or use a personal library, answer Yes.

NoteWhat do these packages do?
  • ragg draws high-quality images of plots.
  • svglite draws sharp, scalable plots.
  • quarto lets R talk to Quarto (it is a helper, not Quarto itself).

You may also be asked to install a few others (such as knitr or rmarkdown). Say yes.

  • Windows
  • Mac
  • Linux

Nothing more to do. If a message mentions Rtools, you can ignore it. These packages install ready-made.

Nothing more to do in most cases. If a pop-up asks to install “command line developer tools,” you can click Install, but you do not need it for these packages.

On Linux, R packages are sometimes built on your machine and need a few system libraries. If the installation stops with an error that names a missing library, install the libraries and try again. On Ubuntu or Debian, this usually does it:

Terminal
sudo apt install libfreetype6-dev libpng-dev libtiff5-dev libjpeg-dev \
  libfontconfig1-dev libharfbuzz-dev libfribidi-dev

On other distributions, the names are similar but slightly different. Searching for the library names in the error message will lead you to the right ones.

Step 4: Check Quarto, Typst, and TinyTeX

apaquarto needs Quarto 1.9 or newer. RStudio comes with its own copy of Quarto, but the copy may be older than that, so we check.

Check what you have

  1. Click the Terminal tab in RStudio.
  2. Type this and press EnterEnter:
Terminal
quarto check

You will see a list of checks. Look for these lines:

  • Quarto version. Does it say 1.9 or higher? If not, install the newest Quarto from the Quarto download page, then close and reopen RStudio and run quarto check again.
  • Typst. Quarto ships with Typst built in, so there is nothing separate to install. Typst is a modern document-typesetting tool that makes .pdf files quickly and without any extra software.
  • TinyTeX (under Checking LaTeX installation). This is only needed for one of the .pdf options (see the next box).
TipDo I need TinyTeX?

apaquarto can make a .pdf in two ways:

Format name How it makes a .pdf Needs TinyTeX?
apaquarto-typst Typst No
apaquarto-pdf LaTeX Yes (unless you already have a LaTeX setup)

If you only want .docx, .html, and a .pdf from Typst, you can skip TinyTeX entirely. If you want the LaTeX .pdf, install it as shown below.

Already use LaTeX? If you have TeX Live, MacTeX, or MiKTeX installed, Quarto will find it. You can skip TinyTeX.

Install or update TinyTeX (optional)

In the Console:

Console
# Install tinytex package
install.packages("tinytex")
# Install TinyTeX program
tinytex::install_tinytex()
# Verify the installation
tinytex::is_tinytex()

This downloads a few hundred megabytes, so it may take several minutes. To update TinyTeX later:

Console
tinytex::tlmgr_update()
NoteIf a pop-up or message asks permission

Quarto may ask whether you want to continue or whether to add TinyTeX to your system path. The defaults are fine. If your computer is managed by your university and an installation is blocked, contact your IT help desk. Please do not try to work around the block yourself.

When you are done, run quarto check once more in the terminal. Everything you plan to use should report as OK.

Step 5: Create an RStudio Project in Your Thesis Folder

An RStudio project is a folder that RStudio treats as home base for one piece of work. When you open the project, everything is right where you left it, and apaquarto, your references, and your figures can all find each other. Do this for your thesis.

Your thesis folder will hold your thesis file, your references, your images, and the finished documents.

ImportantFolder names

Give the folder a short name with no spaces or special characters, such as Dissertation or thesis-2027. Spaces are not forbidden, but they cause trouble more often than you would expect.

Pick the path that matches your situation:

  • A. I want the folder only on my computer
  • B. I already have a folder in OneDrive that is on my computer
  • C. I have a OneDrive folder that is not on my computer yet
  1. In File Explorer or Finder, go to a place you will remember (for example, Documents).
  2. Make a new folder: right-click in an empty spot, then choose New → Folder (Windows) or New Folder (Mac). Name it Dissertation.
  3. In RStudio, choose File → New Project…
  4. Choose Existing Directory.
  5. Click Browse…, select your new folder, click Choose (or Open), then click Create Project.

RStudio reopens inside your project. You should see your folder’s contents in the Files pane.

This is the same as the previous path. Just choose the folder from your OneDrive location when you click Browse… In File Explorer or Finder, it is under OneDrive.

This is a common situation. Your folder exists in OneDrive online, but you have never put a copy on your computer. The solution is to sync it. Syncing tells OneDrive, “keep a copy of this folder on my computer, and keep both copies up to date.”

First, make sure the OneDrive app is installed and signed in with your Temple account.

  • Windows
  • Mac
  • Linux

OneDrive is built into Windows 10 and 11. Look for a cloud icon in the notification area at the bottom right of your screen (you may need to click the small up-arrow). If you do not see it, search the Start menu for OneDrive and sign in with your Temple email address.

  1. Download and install the OneDrive app (or get it from the App Store).
  2. Open it and sign in with your Temple email address.
  3. If macOS asks for permission for OneDrive to access files, allow it. If your Mac asks you to change a setting in System Settings → Privacy & Security, follow the prompt.

Microsoft does not offer an official OneDrive app for Linux. Your best options are to keep the project in a normal local folder (use path A) and upload copies to OneDrive in your browser when you want a backup, or to use a community-made OneDrive client. If you choose the latter, ask your IT contact whether it is permitted on your machine.

Then, sync the folder:

  1. In a web browser, go to OneDrive (through your Temple Microsoft 365 sign-in) and open the folder that contains your thesis folder.
  2. Select the thesis folder and click Sync in the toolbar (it may be under the … menu). If your browser asks to open OneDrive, click Open.
  3. Wait a moment. The folder now appears in File Explorer or Finder under OneDrive.

If your thesis folder does not exist at all yet, make it right in OneDrive’s online view (click New → Folder) and then sync it as above. Or make it inside the OneDrive folder on your computer (see path B) and it will appear online automatically.

Now make it a project, exactly as in path A: File → New Project… → Existing Directory → Browse… and choose the folder that now sits under OneDrive.

WarningTwo things to know about OneDrive
  • “Files On-Demand.” To save space, OneDrive may keep some files only online and download them when you open them. Programs that look inside the folder can be confused by this. Right-click your thesis folder and choose Always keep on this device, so that every file is really there.
  • Syncing while you render. OneDrive sometimes tries to copy a file at the very moment RStudio is writing it, which can cause a brief error. If you see an error about a file being “in use” or “locked,” wait a few seconds and render again. Do not keep two copies of the same project open on two computers at once.

If you notice repeated trouble, the safest arrangement is to keep the project in a plain local folder and copy it to OneDrive when you reach a stopping point. Either way, back up your work regularly, and consider keeping a dated copy of your .qmd file at important milestones.

NoteHow will I know it worked?

The top right corner of RStudio now shows your folder name, and a file ending in .Rproj appears in the Files pane. Next time, you can open your thesis by double-clicking that .Rproj file, or by using File → Recent Projects.

Step 6: Install apaquarto in the Project Folder

An extension is a small package of formatting rules that you add to a project. You install apaquarto into your thesis folder so that Quarto knows how to format it.

  1. Open your project in RStudio (if it is not open already).
  2. Click the Terminal tab. It starts inside your project folder.
  3. Type or paste this, then press EnterEnter:
Terminal
quarto add wjschne/apaquarto
  1. Quarto asks whether you trust the author of the extension. If you do, type Y and press EnterEnter. You are agreeing that the extension may run its formatting code on your computer.
  2. If it asks whether to proceed with installation, answer Y.

A new folder called _extensions appears in your Files pane. Do not edit or delete it. That is where apaquarto lives.

TipPrefer typing in the R Console?

This does the same thing:

Console
quarto::quarto_add_extension("wjschne/apaquarto", no_prompt = TRUE)

Installing the extension happens per project. If you start a different project (for a paper, say), repeat this step there.

Step 7: Create Your Thesis File

  1. In RStudio, choose File → New File → Quarto Document…
  2. In the window that appears, leave everything as it is and click Create Empty Document (or Create).
  3. Choose File → Save. Name the file dissertation.qmd (or thesis.qmd), and save it in your project folder. Be sure the name ends in .qmd.

You now have a file open in the Source pane. Its first few lines might look like this:

---
title: "Untitled"
---

The part between the two lines of three dashes is called the YAML (said “YAM-ul”). It holds your document’s settings: title, author, and so on. Everything after it is your writing. In the next step, we will fill the settings in for you.

NoteSource mode and Visual mode

RStudio has two ways of looking at a Quarto file. Source shows you the plain text, with its markup. Visual looks more like a word processor, with buttons for bold and italics. The two are interchangeable, and you can switch with the Source / Visual buttons at the top of the editor. This tutorial describes Source mode, because it shows you exactly what is happening. Visual mode is a fine choice for ordinary writing, but it can sometimes rewrite unusual bits of markup, so keep an eye on the YAML at the top if you try it.

Step 8: Draft Your Front Matter with apa7maker

Your thesis starts with a title page, copyright page, abstract, dedication, acknowledgments, and tables of contents. apaquarto builds all of these for you from the settings in your YAML. apa7maker is a free web page that writes those settings for you, so you do not have to remember them.

  1. Open apa7maker. Be patient: it can take a little while to load.
  2. Set the document mode to thesis.
  3. Fill in the form: title, your name, the degree, your committee, and so on. Fields you do not need can be left blank.
  4. When you are ready, click Make Document in the side menu, then click Update.
  5. Copy the text that appears.
  6. Back in RStudio, in your dissertation.qmd file, select everything (Ctrl+ACtrl+A, or Cmd+ACmd+A on a Mac), and paste over it (Ctrl+VCtrl+V or Cmd+VCmd+V). Save the file.
ImportantPrivacy

apa7maker runs in your browser. Still, as with any website, avoid typing anything into it that you would not want others to see. Your name, your title, and your committee members’ names are all it needs.

If you would like to see what the finished settings can look like, here is a trimmed example:

---
title: "An Inquiry into Vexing Problems at the Intersection of Digital Typography, Tasseomancy, and Badminton"
shorttitle: "Typography, Tasseomancy, and Badminton"
author:
  - name: Your Name
abstract: "This is my abstract."
dedication: |
  For everyone who wondered whether this would ever be finished.
acknowledgments: |
  Thanks to my committee, my family, and the miracle of coffee.
bibliography: references.yaml
documentmode: thesis
thesis:
  type: Dissertation
  submitted-to: the Temple University Graduate Board
  degree: Doctor of Philosophy
  date: May 2027
  committee:
    - name: Advisor's Name, Ph.D.
      role: Advisory Chair
      affiliation: Department of Your Field
    - name: Committee Member's Name, Ph.D.
      affiliation: Department of Another Field
    - name: Outside Member's Name, Ph.D.
      role: External Member
      affiliation: Another University
format:
  apaquarto-docx: default
  apaquarto-html: default
  apaquarto-pdf: default
  apaquarto-typst: default
---

Below the YAML goes your writing. A very small starting body looks like this:

# Introduction

The body of the dissertation begins here.

# References

::: {#refs}
:::

Each first-level heading (a line starting with a single #) becomes a chapter in thesis mode. The # References heading with the {#refs} block tells apaquarto where to put your reference list.

NoteWhat the format lines mean

The four lines under format: list the kinds of document you can make. apaquarto-docx is a Word file. apaquarto-html is a web page that is quick to produce and good for drafting. apaquarto-typst makes a .pdf through Typst, and apaquarto-pdf makes a .pdf through LaTeX. You can keep all of them or delete the ones you do not want. The next step shows how to choose among them.

Step 9: Render Your Document

Rendering means turning your .qmd file into a finished document, the way “printing” turns a file into paper.

  1. Click the Render button at the top of the editor (or press Ctrl+Shift+KCtrl+Shift+K; on a Mac, Cmd+Shift+KCmd+Shift+K).
  2. If you listed more than one format, click the small arrow beside Render to choose which one to make.
  3. A preview appears in the Viewer pane or in a browser tab, and the finished file appears in your project folder.
TipWhich format should I render?
Format Good for
apaquarto-html Quick drafting. It renders fastest, so you can see the effect of your edits.
apaquarto-docx Sharing with an advisor who wants to comment in Word.
apaquarto-typst A polished and quickly rendered .pdf with no extra software.
apaquarto-pdf A .pdf made through LaTeX (needs TinyTeX or another LaTeX installation). It renders slowly compared to the other formats, but LaTeX is famous for rendering text that has a certain subtle beauty for the discerning eye.

My suggestion: draft with HTML, check the layout occasionally with the Typst .pdf, and render all formats before you send a version to anyone. Check with your Graduate School on which format it will accept for the final submission.

WarningSomething went wrong. What now?
  • Read the message in the Background Jobs tab. It often names the line number and the problem. Common causes are a missing quotation mark or a stray space in the YAML.
  • YAML is picky about spaces. The indentation in the example above is deliberate. Indent with spaces, never tabs.
  • A file is open in Word. If you rendered a .docx and have it open in Word, close it before rendering again.
  • Quarto is too old. Run quarto check again (Step 4).
  • If you are stuck, copy the error message and ask for help in the apaquarto issue tracker.

Step 10: Writing with Markdown

Markdown is a way of writing formatted text using ordinary keyboard characters. It looks odd for about ten minutes, then becomes second nature. You can always look back at this page. For anything not covered here, see the writing page and the Quarto Markdown guide.

Formatting text

You type You get
*italics* italics
**bold** bold
***bold italics*** bold italics
H~2~O H2O (subscript)
x^2^ x2 (superscript)

APA style rarely uses bold in the body of your writing, but you might need it for a term or two. Use italics for emphasis, for statistical symbols such as p, and for the titles of books.

Separating paragraphs

Leave one blank line between paragraphs:

This is my first paragraph. It can run on for as many lines as I like,
and the line breaks in the file do not matter.

This is my second paragraph. The blank line above is what separates them.

apaquarto indents the first line of each paragraph automatically. Do not use the Tab key or spaces to indent.

Headings

Start a line with one to five # symbols, followed by a space. More # means a smaller heading:

# Chapter Title

## Level 2 Heading

### Level 3 Heading

#### Level 4 Heading

##### Level 5 Heading

In thesis mode, a level-1 heading starts a new chapter in all caps. Write headings in title case (capitalize the major words); apaquarto handles the rest of the formatting. See the writing page for how APA headings differ from level to level.

Making tables

The simplest table is drawn with vertical bars and dashes. Give it a label that begins with tbl- and a title after a colon:

| Scale    | Description                    |
|:---------|:-------------------------------|
| Nominal  | Categories without an order    |
| Ordinal  | Ordered categories             |
| Interval | Quantities without a true zero |
| Ratio    | Quantities with a true zero    |

: Classic Scale Types {#tbl-scales apa-note="Adapted from Stevens's classification."}
  • #tbl-scales is the table’s label. In your text, @tbl-scales becomes “Table 1” (and the number updates itself if you add a table earlier!).
  • apa-note is optional and puts a note below the table.
  • The colons in the second row control alignment: :--- left, :---: center, ---: right.

Typing a table by hand is fiddly. If you switch to Visual mode (Step 7), RStudio offers Insert → Table with a friendly grid. You can also build a table in a spreadsheet and copy the layout.

For more advanced users, you can create beautiful and/or complex tables with various packages in R or Python. I wrote the apa7 package, which is designed specifically for creating APA-Style tables. I wrote a 24-part series of blog posts starting here in which I recreated all of the example tables from the APA Manual.

Inserting images

Put the image file in your project folder (for example, in a subfolder named img). Then:

![A Simple Mediation Model](img/mediation.png){#fig-mediation width="5in"
fig-alt="A path diagram in which A causes B, and B causes C."
apa-note="Variable A indirectly causes Variable C."}
  • The text in [ ] is the figure’s title.
  • #fig-mediation is the label: write @fig-mediation in your text to refer to “Figure 1”.
  • fig-alt is a description of the image for readers who use a screen reader. Please always include one. It is good practice and makes your work accessible.
  • width and apa-note are optional.
TipWhat is the difference between a figure and an illustration?

A figure is a chart or graph. An illustration is other artwork, such as a photograph or a map. If you label an image with #ill- instead of #fig-, apaquarto numbers it separately, as in “Illustration 1.” (Check with your committee whether it prefers one scheme.)

Block quotes

A quotation of 40 words or more goes in its own indented block. Start each line with >:

Austen [-@austen1990] had this to say about memory:

> If any one faculty of our nature may be called more wonderful than the rest,
> I do think it is memory. There seems something more speakingly
> incomprehensible in the powers, the failures, the inequalities of memory,
> than in any other of our intelligences. (p. 163)

For a quotation that ends in an attribution, as an epigraph often does, put a final line that starts with three hyphens (---), which turn into an em dash:

> We are, to be sure, a miracle every way.
>
> --- Jane Austen, *Mansfield Park* [-@austen1990]

If a paragraph continues after the block quote (it is the same paragraph, not a new one), wrap the continuation so it is not indented:

:::{.NoIndent}
And that is why memory matters.
:::

Appendices

Appendices come after the references. Each one is a level-1 heading with a label that starts with apx-:

# References

::: {#refs}
:::

# Interview Questions {#apx-interview}

The questions asked of each participant are in this appendix.

# Survey Instrument {#apx-survey}

The full survey is reproduced here.
  • You do not need to write the word “Appendix.” apaquarto adds it, plus the letters: Appendix A, Appendix B, and so on. In thesis mode, appendices are also gathered in the table of contents.
  • In your text, @apx-interview becomes “Appendix A” (or whichever letter it was given).
  • Tables and figures inside appendices are numbered automatically: Table A1, Figure B1, and so forth.

Step 11: Set Up Zotero for Citations

Zotero is a free reference manager. You collect your sources in it (articles, books, chapters), and it remembers every detail of each source. apaquarto then reads Zotero’s list so that citations in your text and your reference list are built automatically, in APA style. You never type a reference by hand, and you never have to fix one by hand either.

11a. Install Zotero

Download Zotero from zotero.org/download and install it. It is available for Windows, Mac, and Linux. (Linux users get an archive to unpack; Zotero’s download page links to instructions.)

You will also want the Zotero Connector, a browser add-on from the same page. When you are looking at an article on a website, one click on the connector adds it to your Zotero library.

11b. Install Better BibTeX

Better BibTeX is a Zotero add-on. It does two things you need: it gives each source a short, stable citation key (like austen1990), and it can export your library to a file that stays up to date.

  1. Go to the Better BibTeX installation page and download the latest .xpi file. (On some browsers you need to right-click the download link and choose Save Link As… so the browser does not try to open it.)
  2. In Zotero, choose Tools → Plugins (on some versions and systems, Tools → Add-ons).
  3. Click the gear icon near the top right and choose Install Plugin From File… (or Install Add-on From File…).
  4. Select the .xpi file you downloaded.
  5. Restart Zotero if asked.

11c. Add your sources

The quickest way to add a source is with its DOI (for articles and many chapters) or ISBN (for books):

  1. In Zotero, click the magic wand icon in the toolbar, called Add Item(s) by Identifier.
  2. Type or paste a DOI (such as 10.1037/amp0000700) or an ISBN, and press EnterEnter.
  3. Zotero looks up all the details and adds the source to your library.
TipAlways glance at the result

Automatic lookups are good, but not perfect. Click each new source and check that the authors, year, title, and journal look right. Mistakes in Zotero turn into mistakes in your reference list. Pay particular attention to capitalization of titles and to organization names listed as authors.

For sources without a DOI or ISBN (reports, web pages, unpublished work), use the Zotero Connector while you are on the webpage, or click the New Item button (the green plus) and fill in the fields by hand.

11d. Find your citation key

Click any source. In the right-hand pane, you will see a Citation Key field. That is the short name you will use to cite the source. A key might look like austenMansfieldPark1990. You can leave the keys as they are. Just copy them as you need them.

NoteIs there an easier way to copy a key?

Yes. In Zotero, right-click a source and choose Better BibTeX → Copy Citation Key (the menu wording can differ slightly across versions). Then paste it into your document.

11e. Export your library to your project folder

Now you will make a file that holds your library, in your project folder, so that Quarto can read it. You do this once, and the file keeps itself up to date.

  1. In Zotero, right-click your library (My Library) or the collection of sources for your thesis in the left-hand pane.
  2. Choose Export Library… (or Export Collection…).
  3. Set Format to Better CSL YAML.
  4. Check the box that says “Keep updated.”
  5. Click OK, and in the file dialog, go to your thesis project folder (the one from Step 5). Name the file references.yaml and click Save.

You will now see references.yaml in the RStudio Files pane. From now on, whenever you add or change a source in Zotero, the file updates itself, as long as Zotero is open.

WarningIf you work in a OneDrive folder

Save the file inside the synced folder (see Step 5) so that it travels with the project. If you see a message about the file being in use, wait a few seconds and let OneDrive finish syncing.

11f. Tell your document where the references are

In your .qmd file’s YAML (the part at the top), make sure there is a bibliography line that names the file:

bibliography: references.yaml

If you used apa7maker in Step 8, it may have a place to enter this. If not, add the line yourself, and make sure it is aligned with the other top-level lines, with no leading spaces. You do not need to supply a citation style: apaquarto already includes APA style.

Step 12: Cite Sources in the Text

You cite a source by writing @ followed by its citation key. When you render, apaquarto turns it into the right citation and adds the source to your reference list. Only sources you actually cite appear in the references.

I will use @smith2020 and @jones2019 as stand-ins for your own keys.

Parenthetical citations

Put the citation in square brackets. This is the form you use at the end of a sentence:

You type You get
[@smith2020] (Smith, 2020)
[@smith2020; @jones2019] (Jones, 2019; Smith, 2020)
[@smith2020, pp. 35--41] (Smith, 2020, 35-41)
[e.g., @smith2020] (e.g., Smith, 2020)
[see @smith2020, p. 12] (see Smith, 2020, 12)

A few details:

  • Several sources: separate them with a semicolon. They are alphabetized for you.
  • Several authors: apaquarto handles the ampersand and “et al.” according to APA rules. You do nothing.
  • Page numbers: type a single page or a page range with a hyphen between the first and last page number. There is not need to add p. or pp., because apaquarto will add that for you. Also, the hyphen automatically will be converted to an en dash (–), as specified by APA style.
  • A prefix such as e.g. or see goes just before the @. If you have a prefix for a group of sources, attach it to the citation that comes first alphabetically.

Example in a sentence:

Self-efficacy predicts persistence [e.g., @jones2019, @smith2020, 35-41].

Narrative citations

A narrative citation puts the author’s name in your sentence. Write the key with no brackets:

You type You get
@smith2020 found a result. Smith (2020) found a result.
@smith2020 [101--103] found a result. Smith (2020, pp. 101–103) found a result.
@smith2020 ['s] theory Smith’s (2020) theory
@smith2020 ['s, 35] theory Smith’s (2020, p. 35) theory

Notes:

  • For page numbers, put them in square brackets after the key. You do not need to type “p.” or “pp.” here; apaquarto adds it, and a hyphen between two numbers becomes an en dash.
  • The ['s] trick is an apaquarto feature for possessives. It puts the possessive on the author’s name and moves the year outside, as APA prescribes.
TipTip: Make a citation easier to spot

If a citation does not turn into a name and year, but shows up as @smith2020 in your output, the key is not in your references file. Check the spelling, and check that Zotero has the source and has exported it (Step 11e). Capitalization matters!

For more patterns (suppressing ampersands, masked citations, meta-analysis references), see the citations section of the writing page.

Step 13: Changing Options Later

You can change how your thesis looks at any time by changing its YAML settings. There are two ways:

Change the YAML directly

Open your .qmd file and edit the settings at the top. For example, to change the committee or the date:

thesis:
  date: December 2027

or to change the left margin:

margin:
  left: 1.25in

The options page lists everything you can change, including the thesis options. Each entry includes an example you can copy.

Or use apa7maker again

Go back to apa7maker, import your document, change what you like, click Make Document and Update, and copy the YAML. Then replace only the YAML at the top of your file (the part between the two lines of three dashes), leaving your writing below untouched.

ImportantBe careful about the margins and layout

Thesis mode sets margins, spacing, fonts, and page numbering to follow the Graduate School’s handbook. Changing them can make your thesis stop conforming. If you change them, confirm with the Graduate School before submitting.

Troubleshooting Quick List

Problem Try this
RStudio cannot find R Reinstall R (Step 1), then restart RStudio.
quarto is “not recognized” in the Terminal Close and reopen RStudio. If that fails, reinstall Quarto (quarto.org).
The extension did not install Make sure the Terminal is inside your project folder, then repeat Step 6.
The .pdf (LaTeX) will not render Run quarto check, then quarto install tinytex (Step 4). Or use apaquarto-typst.
A citation shows as @something The key is missing from references.yaml or is misspelled (Step 12).
A figure does not appear Check the file name, its extension, and that the image sits where you said it does.
An error about a file in use Close the output file, wait for OneDrive to finish syncing, and render again.
